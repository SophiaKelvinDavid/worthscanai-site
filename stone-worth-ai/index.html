Collector-significant examples

Coins where the exact details matter

These real photographed coins show why date, mint, variety, and condition must be resolved before discussing value. Collector interest does not guarantee that a similar-looking coin is rare or valuable.

Key date · First year of issue

1909-S VDB Lincoln Cent

Why collectors care: A famous key issue from the Lincoln cent's first year, identified by its San Francisco mint mark and designer initials.

What to notice
The “S” below the date and “VDB” initials at the bottom of the wheat reverse.
Market significance
Major collector interest; authentication is especially important.
Photo: Lost Dutchman Rare Coins / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0 US
Key date · Denver issue

1916-D Mercury Dime

Why collectors care: The low-mintage Denver issue is the recognized key date of the Mercury dime series.

What to notice
The 1916 date and small “D” mint mark on the reverse near the lower-left of the fasces.
Market significance
Widely collected, scarce, and frequently merits expert authentication.
Photo: United States Mint / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0
Key date · San Francisco issue

1893-S Morgan Dollar

Why collectors care: This scarce San Francisco issue is a defining key date in the Morgan dollar series.

What to notice
The 1893 date; confirm the “S” mint mark on the reverse and assess surfaces carefully.
Market significance
Strong, long-established collector demand makes authenticity and condition critical.
Photo: Bruxton / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 4.0
First-year type · Silver issue

1932 Washington Quarter

Why collectors care: The first year of the Washington quarter anchors a long-running series; its branch-mint issues are especially sought after.

What to notice
The 1932 date, then check the reverse for no mint mark, “D,” or “S.”
Market significance
Mint attribution separates the scarcer 1932-D and 1932-S from the Philadelphia issue.
Photo: Numismatic Guaranty Corporation / Wikimedia Commons · U.S. public domain
Familiar comparison · 1976 issue

Bicentennial Half Dollar

Why collectors care: A recognizable special design that shows how historical interest and rarity are not the same thing.

What to notice
The 1776–1976 dual date, reverse design, mint mark, and clad or silver composition.
Market significance
Widely saved; exact format and condition matter more than the dual date alone.
Photo: RHM22 / Wikimedia Commons · CC BY-SA 3.0

Important notes

A smart starting point, with honest limits

AI-assisted analysis can help you research a find, but it is not a professional appraisal, laboratory identification, authentication, or grading service.

What can affect a result

  • Photo quality and lighting
  • Wear or damage
  • Unusual variants
  • Incomplete visible evidence

When to verify

For potentially high-value stones or coins, seek professional verification. Valuable coins may also warrant professional authentication or grading.
